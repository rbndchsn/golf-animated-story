# Research and evidentiary labeling

This story is a dramatic interpretation of a working hypothesis about golf as a symbolic sexual and fertility ritual, with an additional historical inquiry into the conditions of child caddying. It is not a finding that golf was founded for a sexual purpose, that Freemasonry orchestrated abuse, or that early golf clubs operated a trafficking system.

## Four visible classifications

**HISTORICAL RECORD**: A claim derived from a reference, such as the 1764 reduction of St Andrews from 22 holes per round to 18, historical child caddying, or the 1909 Acland Report. The source still needs to be judged for quality and quotation accuracy.

**SYMBOLIC READING**: An analogy proposed by the hypothesis, for example the club as phallus, a golf swing as sexual release, the ball as egg, the hole as nest, and the scoring bird as imagined offspring. An internally coherent metaphor is not proof that it was historically intentional.

**RECORD + INTERPRETATION**: Both a documented etymology or usage and an inference, for example the historical Scots word *burd* for woman juxtaposed with the independent American golf origin of *birdie*.

**OPEN QUESTION**: A research question, unverified alleged pattern, or missing historical record, including the most serious allegation concerning possible organized sexual exploitation or trafficking of young caddies.

## Especially important distinctions

- Children from poor families worked as caddies, sometimes as young as seven, eight, or nine according to a 1902 report cited by later historians. It does not follow that *all* golf clubs employed children that young.
- Residential caddie camps existed in some early twentieth-century North American settings. That does not establish overnight residence for the seven-to-nine-year-old British caddies mentioned in the 1902 report.
- Some influential early golfers had documented Freemason affiliations. That does not establish a Masonic origin for the symbolic fertility reading or wrongdoing by the institution.
- A later convicted adult golfer exploited access to a young caddie. That establishes the reality of an individual abuse case, not an organized nineteenth-century trafficking network.
- Adult same-sex attraction or homoerotic symbolism is not evidence of child sexual abuse. These topics must not be conflated.
- The absence of an identified early sexual-abuse prosecution is not evidence that no abuse occurred, but it does mean the proposed early organized pattern remains unverified.

## Archive priorities

Investigate school inspector reports, the primary 1909 Acland text, Wray Vamplew's study of caddies, local club employment ledgers, the historical Great Yarmouth / Coventry correspondence about the Bogey Man, visitors' rules, caddie recruiting arrangements, police/court files, parent complaints, and camp board records. For every case, retain exact dates, archive accession details, contemporaneous wording, and a distinction between allegation, charge, conviction, and speculation.
