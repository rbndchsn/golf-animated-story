# THE GAME BENEATH THE GAME?

## A frame-by-frame clubhouse conversation and illustrated research storyboard

*Companion to the PowerPoint and interactive web comic. Adapted from the research dossier `Golf_hypothesis_v1.md` in the GOLF Google Drive folder.*

**Format:** 43 illustrated conversation scenes, five main acts, prologue and epilogue. 
**Characters:** **Quinn**, the Questioner (gold accent), and **Vale**, the Archivist (teal accent).
**Narrative arc:** curiosity → linguistic and reproductive interpretation → male rivalry and clubs → child labor and unequal power → investigation and evidence standards.

### Editorial position

The symbolic reading, including its sexual and reproductive imagery, is deliberately retained and voiced strongly. This script does **not** represent the reading as a verified origin of golf; nor does it present the Freemasonry or organized child trafficking hypothesis as an established historical finding. Sexual orientation is explicitly distinguished from sexual abuse. The scenes with children are documentary, never sexualized.

### How to use

- **PPTX:** Click forward one slide at a time. The speakers, dialogue, pop-up conclusion, and citations are editable slide elements; artwork is embedded as a cartoon image. Presenter notes include the longer context and source URLs.
- **HTML:** Open the single `.html` file in a web browser. Click “Reveal” to bring up the scene-specific popup; click “Next” to proceed. Use arrow keys or Space, and open the Notes/Sources panel for details. The file works offline.
- **Research:** Use the cited dossier and original sources before publishing factual claims, especially the allegations of earlier coordinated abuse.

### Evidence legend

- **FACT:** Historical record: documented in the cited sources, or a specific attested linguistic term.
- **LENS:** Symbolic reading: interpretive hypothesis, not a demonstrated etymology or historical intention.
- **MIX:** Record and reading: documentation plus an interpretation that must be kept distinct.
- **OPEN:** Open question: researchable but not currently established by the cited record.

## Scene 23 — Par was already on the board

**ACT III · COMPETITION · FACT**

**Quinn:** Wait: was there no par before the Bogey Man?

**Vale:** The word par was in golf use by about 1870. Bogey originally described another target score, not one over par.

**Visual pop-up:** PAR ≠ ORIGINAL BOGEY. THE MODERN RELATION CAME LATER.

**Illustration cue:** `par`. The scene is drawn in restrained editorial stick-figure line art; only adults are used in sexual-symbolic discussion.

The word par had entered golf usage before the imaginary bogey competitor. In the older game, bogey was a specified good score to beat or tie; later par standards led to bogey being defined as one over par. A stable expected number was always possible without a spooky rival.

## Further research

For historical caddie systems, prioritize club minutes, admission and wage books, parent/guardian information, campsite rules, dormitory rosters, correspondence, police files, court records, and oral testimony. Verify institutional permissions and the exact ages of the children in each specific program. Do not assume that the school inspector’s reports of 7–9-year-olds establish their overnight residence. Distinguish allegations, charges, convictions and later retellings.
